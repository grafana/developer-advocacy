## Summary

The show is titled "Grafana ObservabilityCON 26 London: Building an Observable Enterprise for AI Driven Operations," published on November 12, 2026. The speakers, Durk and Christian, are from Bayer and discussed their journey of implementing Grafana's observability solutions in a bring-your-own-cloud environment. They highlighted the importance of end-to-end observability for managing thousands of applications, integrating Grafana tools like the Grafana Assistant and Alloy, and transitioning to a self-service model for application teams. Key takeaways include the need for effective monitoring and automation to enhance operational efficiency and the challenges of integrating observability across a complex IT landscape.

Topics: Grafana, Observability, AI, Cloud, Automation, Monitoring, Bayer

Now, let’s discuss our move to Bring Your Own Cloud and the Grafana platform, which is the reason we are here today. Our journey began at the start of 2025 when we underwent a reorganization in IT. Our department was tasked with managing and operating a few thousand applications, which was a new responsibility. 

In a company like ours, many traditional applications run on a legacy IT stack that needs to be maintained. These applications don’t change much in terms of functionality; they just need to run stably. In contrast, we also have cloud-native applications that have a very high change and deployment rate. To allow our team to focus on developing new applications, we decided to create a specific environment for the legacy applications to ensure they run stably.

Our task was to find a reliable and stable way to manage this. We realized that we needed to remediate all the issues quickly, as the legacy IT environment is critical to the company. Resolving any issues fast and finding the root cause was essential. However, it was also important to ensure that we could scale our solutions across the entire enterprise, not just for isolated cases. 

If we could scale quickly, it would free up resources and engineering capacity to focus on new developments rather than operating traditional applications. This realization led us to change our approach significantly. 

We recognized that we lacked sufficient information to assess what was happening in our landscape. We had signals, alerts, and everything required to identify and solve issues quickly, but we needed to observe the environment end-to-end. This was our first step, and we began our journey with the platform by looking at providers and partners to help us achieve this.

We wanted to implement this observability for the entire enterprise, not just for a single application. This included backend systems, backup systems, networks, and firewalls—all of which are connected to the workloads and can impact performance. Correlating all of this information is crucial for finding root causes. 

Finally, we wanted to automate our responses, implementing self-healing scripts or whatever it takes to ensure efficiency. This approach was presented to our management, and we received the endorsement to start this journey in a more practical way.

This is now more of a driven contribution model than anything else. The dimension on top, which Durk already mentioned, is that we want to drive this forward. We are now laying the ecosystem on that and activating it. As soon as agent observability came into play, we began to think about how to perform system reboots simply and how to change the sizes of machines. We need to monitor not only the systems but also the agents, understanding the reasoning behind that.

There are also jurisdictions that are driving us to document this thought process. This is now done within our IT ecosystem with agent observability. We are increasingly extending this system to our business divisions. Crop science and pharmaceuticals are also starting to use agentic solutions. To be fair, this is quite an early stage for our industry because we are highly regulated. As we all know, agents, hallucination issues, and non-deterministic aspects are core challenges in our business. Getting AI to perform deterministic tasks is what we are setting Grafana as a key aspect of.

As the next steps, we want to integrate the Grafana Assistant into our own agentic ecosystem so that it can communicate with every agent we have and trigger actions in the systems. The aspects of data integration into Grafana are also crucial. 

Was that journey smooth to this point? Before we move on, I want to make one comment. What we don't have on the slides, but is also very important, is that we started with our initial scope, which I just outlined, involving a few thousand applications. This added another task to our plate: to develop a contribution model so that we could onboard others who could benefit from the data we had collected. 

From a change management perspective, we have to say this has been one of the easier projects in IT that we have ever undertaken. It’s a self-selling business, to be very honest. With all the advantages of the Assistant doing dashboarding and other tasks, we have lowered the hurdle to a level that everyone can digest. We are currently working on integration with Teams so that everyone who has access rights in Grafana can engage in direct dialogue.

We are now utilizing the deep investigation functionality of the Incident Response Management (IRM) system, triggering automatic deep investigations for all incidents. This is just the first step of the journey. We want to start with simple tasks like increasing disk space or clearing block files automatically, all triggered with webhooks or agentic integrations to find the right solution for the right system. Every system for us is a bit different, and this presents a tough challenge. Security is becoming increasingly important. We have a multifaceted and quite interesting identity and access management concept in our company. 

This is where the integration regarding the identities of agents becomes really interesting. We are recognizing that we are at an early stage of technology and thinking that we are in a legacy situation. 

On this journey, we still have some challenges. One of the biggest things regarding our contribution model is that we wanted to adopt a single stack approach. We know many of our followers, competitors, or even partners are using different approaches with multistack systems. We have definitely stated that we never want to find ourselves in a siloed situation again. We want to achieve correlation across everything and go the extra mile in doing RB and LBK, getting the data into one single system and extracting value from it through correlation functionality.

I must point out that not every feature is Arbuck enabled and Albug enabled as we would like it to be. This capability allows us to have all the signals, alerts, metrics, logs—whatever it takes—at hand. This enables us to act on it and correlate the data, which is very important.

Every technical issue that is reported by a user should be taken and transformed into something that is observable. At the end, you can measure how many technical issues are still being reported. If something technical happens within the system that affects user experience, you can observe it and potentially recognize it before the user is impacted.

This is an important aspect because we need insights about the data. We also want to work on the standards regarding the telemetry data we have, so we can correlate everything. We have pointed this out quite a bit and often, as it is key.

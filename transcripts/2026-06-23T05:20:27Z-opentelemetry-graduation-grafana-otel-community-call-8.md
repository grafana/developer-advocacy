Baggage has been around since the beginning, but it's never really been highlighted. The point of baggage actually is not just about telemetry. People are starting to talk about baggage now in the context of telemetry, but the original point of baggage is one of the reasons why tracing was never really broadly adopted. It's so much work to build a tracing system that propagates context. 

To make any kind of tracing work, you have to have a little bag of attributes: your trace ID, your span ID, and some other things. You need to be able to have that follow the flow of execution through not just your computer process, but whenever there's a network call, that information has to get serialized somewhere and sent to the next hop, and then deserialized and put into a thread-local or somewhere where it gets propagated along. That's a ton of work. 

Even though tracing is really useful context for logging and other things, people didn't really adopt it because it was just so hard to build that from scratch and get it out there. So, the point of baggage was to say, "Hey, now that we've done all the work to build this context propagation system, are there other kinds of cross-cutting concerns that might want to use this other than just telemetry?" 

Security is another big example, where people might have security rules or information about whether data is tainted or clean.
